What changes hands, and what does not

The difference between selling a dataset here and sending one over.

Sending a copy
  • The buyer receives a copy; the seller keeps one too
  • Nothing records that the sale happened
  • Either side can later deny the terms
  • A dispute comes down to emails and invoices
  • The seller can sell the same data again tomorrow
Transferring ownership here
  • Ownership moves at the storage layer; no copy is shipped
  • Both parties sign before anything moves
  • A certificate is issued naming both parties and the price
  • The seller's credentials provably stop verifying
  • Anyone can check the certificate without an account

How a sale completes

Four steps, in the order a registrar would run them.

Step 1

Terms are raised

The buyer accepts a listing. An agreement records the asset, the price, the payment reference and any conditions.

Step 2

The seller signs

The current holder signs the terms. Ownership has not moved yet — it stays where it is until the buyer countersigns.

Step 3

The buyer countersigns

Only now does the protocol run: the seller's authority is cancelled, the buyer's is written in, and the storage provider re-seals every block.

Step 4

A certificate is issued

The deed records both signatures, the price, and proof that the buyer can verify the data while the seller no longer can.
