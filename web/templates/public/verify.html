What this page settles

The dispute this register exists to prevent, and how a certificate ends it.

The problem

A hospital sells an imaging archive to an AI company. Months later it says the sale never happened, or that it was never paid. Both sides hold copies of the data and a folder of email. Nothing on the internet records who owns a dataset, so the argument has no factual floor.

What a certificate shows

The deed names both parties as legal entities, records the price and payment reference, and carries both signatures applied before anything moved. It also carries the finding that settles the matter: at the moment of sale, the seller's own credentials stopped verifying the dataset. A seller denying the sale has to explain that record.

What it does not claim

This register attests to what the protocol observed and to what each party signed. It records identity to the standard shown on the certificate, and payment by reference rather than by settling funds itself. It does not adjudicate who held title before the transfer it records — in the same way a domain registrar produces authoritative records without deciding trademark disputes.
