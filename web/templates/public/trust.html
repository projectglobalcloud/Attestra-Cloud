The threat model

We assume the storage provider is semi-trusted: it follows the protocol but has every commercial incentive to cut corners — and may cooperate with other parties after a transaction. We assume buyers and sellers act in their own interest. We assume goodwill nowhere.

Scenario 01 Post-sale collusion Adversary: buyer + storage provider

After a transaction, the acquiring party cooperates with the provider to fabricate possession proofs for data outside the deal. Legacy transfer schemes fall here; Attestra removes the leverage entirely.

Scenario 02 Key substitution Adversary: rogue partner organization

An adversary crafts its registration from an honest organization's key to claim joint custody. Attestra binds every registration to its holder, closing the impersonation path.

Scenario 03 Silent tampering Adversary: the storage layer itself

The provider alters or discards blocks. Any challenged alteration fails verification deterministically; sampling makes escaping routine checks statistically untenable.

How the platform is engineered

Keys never touch disk Organization signing keys are generated in-process and held in secure memory only. A restart retires them; there is nothing on disk to steal.
41-case correctness suite Honest operation, every failure path, and the security-critical invariants that future changes must never break — all pinned by tests.
Peer-reviewed foundation The verification engine implements a design published in a 2025 IEEE journal, independently implemented and validated end to end.
Measured, not promised Every performance number on this site is a live measurement from the deployment you're using, charted in the console.
Synthetic data only The catalog contains no real personal, clinical or financial records — demonstrations carry zero privacy risk.
Transparent limitations Demo sizing, backend trade-offs and deviations from the published design are documented, not hidden.
