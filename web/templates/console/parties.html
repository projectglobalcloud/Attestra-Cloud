What a register entry is for

The protocol proves that a set of keys controls a dataset. It has nothing to say about who those keys belong to in law. A register entry closes that gap: it binds a protocol-level owner identifier to a named entity, its registration number and the jurisdiction it answers to, so that when a deed is later read by someone outside this platform there is an actual counterparty on the page.

Signing keys are generated per party and held in custody by the registry. That is the same arrangement an electronic-signature provider operates under, and every certificate states it plainly rather than implying a stronger guarantee than is being given.

A group of individuals is registered like a shared repository: whoever creates it is its owner, and collaborators are admitted by email address. The distinction matters cryptographically as well as legally — when a group holds a dataset it is the members who are the co-owners, each with their own key, so admitting or removing one person does not re-key anybody else. A company, by contrast, is one legal person and signs as one.
